# Causal/Correlation Discovery, Gap Filling, and Virtual Sensors for Research Vessel Underway Data

Scope: methods to (a) autonomously find relationships across ship datasets (met, TSG, fluorometer, ADCP, nav, ship motion, engine/hotel), (b) fill gaps, (c) build virtual (soft) sensors used for gap filling and as drift references; plus external references (reanalysis/satellite). Plug-in points for OpenRVDAS (readers -> transforms -> writers). Research date: 2026-11-17.

Labeling convention: "Cited Findings" are only from pages opened in this session. "Inferences" contains reasoning and some background knowledge that was NOT verified against an opened page in this session; those items are marked [background, unverified].

## Q1. Causal discovery for time series (Granger, PCMCI/PCMCI+/LPCMCI, Tigramite, transfer entropy, CCM, VARLiNGAM, DYNOTEARS): evidence, failure modes on moving ships

### Takeaway
Constraint-based time-series causal discovery (PCMCI family in Tigramite) is the most mature, Earth-science-oriented toolset, but every method rests on assumptions (causal stationarity, no hidden confounders, faithfulness) that a moving ship violates by construction (location, diurnal cycle, cruise track and ship operations are all confounders). On ship data it is realistic as a hypothesis generator and as a "which links are absent" screen, not as an autonomous source of causal claims.

### Cited Findings
- Runge et al. 2019 (Nature Communications, "Inferring causation from time series in Earth system sciences") argue correlation and regression remain the dominant tools in Earth science even though they are "ambiguous to interpret and often lead to incorrect conclusions"; observational causal inference is proposed as a complement to rarely feasible experiments. — [Runge et al. 2019, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10105-3)
- The same paper lists challenges directly relevant to underway data: strong autocorrelation and time delays; nonlinearity and state dependence; mixing of multiple time scales; non-Gaussian heavy-tailed noise; unobserved drivers making links spurious (they note that identifying the ABSENCE of a link is the more robust finding); nonstationarity from omitted drivers; time aggregation that can "let causal dependencies appear contemporaneous or even cyclic"; measurement error; and selection bias where missingness is causally related to the measured process. — [Runge et al. 2019, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10105-3)
- Method characterizations from that paper: Granger causality is classically linear-autoregressive, "limited to lagged causal dependencies," cannot handle contemporaneous links, and bivariate versions produce unphysical links (Walker circulation example); transfer entropy is its information-theoretic, nonlinear analog; CCM rests on Takens' theorem and needs a recoverable deterministic attractor; PCMCI was developed for "high-dimensional time-lagged linear and nonlinear causal discovery" and accounts for autocorrelation; network-learning algorithms identify graphs only up to a Markov equivalence class; uncertainty estimation is "a major open problem." — [Runge et al. 2019, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10105-3)
- CauseMe (causeme.net) is a benchmark platform with synthetic models mimicking real data challenges (known ground truth) and a call for real datasets with confidently known causal structure; the authors name "the lack of a reliable benchmark database" as a major impediment. — [Runge et al. 2019, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10105-3)
- Tigramite (GPL-3.0, shown as version 5.2 on GitHub) states assumptions per method, beyond Causal Markov and Faithfulness: PCMCI needs causal stationarity, no contemporaneous causal links, no hidden variables; PCMCI+ needs causal stationarity and no hidden variables (outputs a CPDAG with contemporaneous links); LPCMCI needs only causal stationarity (allows latent confounders, outputs a PAG); RPCMCI learns regime-dependent graphs (no contemporaneous links, no hidden variables); J-PCMCI+ handles multiple datasets with context variables, tolerating confounding only if it is context-related. — [Tigramite GitHub](https://github.com/jakobrunge/tigramite)
- Tigramite conditional independence tests: ParCorr (linear, Gaussian), RobustParCorr (robust marginals), ParCorrWLS (heteroskedastic), GPDC/GPDCtorch (additive nonlinear), CMIknn (general, permutation-based), Gsquared and CMIsymb (discrete), RegressionCI and CMIknnMixed (mixed types), plus a CausalEffects class for effect/mediation estimation given a graph; missing values and masks are listed as supported features. — [Tigramite GitHub](https://github.com/jakobrunge/tigramite)
- Note: the PCMCI paper itself (Runge et al. 2019 Science Advances, doi 10.1126/sciadv.aau4996) could not be opened in this session (science.org returned an error; PMC mirror blocked by a captcha), so its quantitative power/false-positive claims are not cited here.
- Sugihara et al. 2012 (Science) CCM: tests whether two series belong to the same dynamical system by cross-mapping shadow manifolds; argues Granger causality assumes separability and is not applicable to nonseparable, weakly coupled deterministic systems; convergence of cross-map skill with library length L is the necessary signature of causation. Caveats from the paper: strong forcing (synchrony) must be ruled out before bidirectional convergence implies coupling; noise and finite L limit convergence; a variable (e.g. SST) may be a proxy for a group of drivers. Example: sardine and anchovy do not interact directly but both cross-map with SST (common driver). — [Sugihara et al. 2012 PDF](https://www.science.ntu.edu.tw/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/causalityCCM.pdf)
- DYNOTEARS (Pamfil et al., AISTATS 2020) is score-based: it learns contemporaneous and time-lagged links by minimizing a penalized loss under a smooth algebraic acyclicity constraint (continuous optimization rather than combinatorial search); authors report it outperforms competitors on simulated data, with growing advantage in high dimensions, and apply it to finance and molecular biology. — [Pamfil et al. 2020, PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/pamfil20a.html)

### Inferences
- Ship-specific confounders map onto Tigramite assumptions: (1) the ship's position is a hidden driver of almost every water/air property, so "TSG salinity causes fluorescence" style links will appear when the ship crosses a front; (2) the diurnal cycle drives air temperature, radiation, SST skin effects and fluorescence quenching jointly; (3) operational state (on station vs. transit, speed, heading changes) drives flow distortion in winds, bubble entrainment in the seawater intake, ADCP bottom/beam quality, and engine/hotel loads. These violate "causal stationarity" and "no hidden variables" unless explicitly modeled.
- Practical mitigation within Tigramite: include ship state variables (speed over ground, heading, distance to front/coast, solar elevation, latitude/longitude or water mass class) as observed nodes or as J-PCMCI+ context variables; use RPCMCI or masks to separate regimes (station vs transit); use the mask feature to exclude flagged/bad data rather than imputing before discovery (imputation before discovery would inject the imputer's assumptions into the graph). This is reasoning, not a tested ship workflow.
- The most defensible autonomous output on ship data is a ranked list of candidate lagged dependencies plus explicit "no detectable link given conditioning set" statements (consistent with Runge et al.'s point that absence of links is more robust), presented to scientists as hypotheses.
- Engine/hotel and ship motion links (e.g. engine RPM -> intake temperature warming, pitch/roll -> ADCP error velocity, heading relative to wind -> anemometer bias) are where causal discovery is most likely to be both correct and actionable, because the physical mechanism is known and testable, and the confounding structure is simpler than for biogeochemistry. Speculative.
- VARLiNGAM [background, unverified]: a linear non-Gaussian structural VAR (Hyvarinen et al. 2010) available in the `lingam` Python package; can orient contemporaneous links using non-Gaussianity, but assumes linearity, no hidden confounders, and stationarity. Not opened this session.
- Granger and transfer entropy implementations exist in statsmodels (grangercausalitytests) and in IDTxl/JIDT [background, unverified].
- Compute: PCMCI with ParCorr on ~10 to 50 variables and a few thousand time steps is laptop-scale; CMIknn and GPDC tests scale much worse because they are permutation- or GP-based [background, unverified; consistent with Tigramite marking CMIknn as permutation-based]. This makes linear tests feasible shipboard and nonlinear tests better suited to shore or overnight batch.

- Quantitative PCMCI performance claims (detection power, false positive control under autocorrelation) from the Science Advances paper were not verified because the page could not be opened.
- No published application of PCMCI/CCM/DYNOTEARS to research-vessel underway multi-sensor data (met + TSG + fluorometer + ADCP + engine) was found in this session. Treat ship applications as speculative.
- LPCMCI (Gerhardus and Runge 2020 NeurIPS) and PCMCI+ (Runge 2020 UAI) original papers were not opened; only Tigramite's assumption table is cited.
- CauseMe current leaderboard results (which methods win under which challenges) were not retrieved.

## Q2. Large-scale correlation mining (MIC and critiques, lagged cross-correlation, mutual information, FDR, surrogate tests) and how to present findings without fooling scientists

### Takeaway
MIC (Reshef 2011) is not the right default: its central "equitability" claim was rebutted and it is less powerful and much slower than alternatives. A realistic autonomous scan is lagged correlation or mutual information or distance correlation across variable pairs, with autocorrelation-aware significance (surrogates or effective-sample-size correction), multiple-testing control, and mandatory conditioning on ship position/time-of-day before anything is shown to a scientist.

### Cited Findings
- Kinney and Atwal (PNAS 2014, arXiv 1301.7745) show Reshef et al.'s equitability claim for MIC was supported only by simulation, that no non-trivial dependence measure can satisfy the R^2-based equitability definition, propose "self-equitability" (from the Data Processing Inequality), and show MIC violates self-equitability and the DPI while mutual information satisfies it. They argue apparent MIC equitability reflected random fluctuations and that the bias reported for MI came from a Kraskov estimator with an inappropriate smoothing parameter. MIC ran about 600 times slower than MI estimation in their tests. — [Kinney and Atwal, arXiv 1301.7745](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.7745)
- The same paper notes Simon and Tibshirani and Gorfine et al. found MIC less powerful than distance correlation (dCor), and suggests dCor or HHG when MI estimation is impractical, while noting they also violate self-equitability. — [Kinney and Atwal, arXiv 1301.7745](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.7745)
- Runge et al. 2019: correlation/regression analyses are often ambiguous and lead to incorrect conclusions; bivariate methods produce unphysical links when common drivers are unconditioned; time aggregation can create spurious contemporaneous or cyclic dependencies; measurement error and selection bias plague observational data. — [Runge et al. 2019, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10105-3)
- Sugihara et al. 2012 cautions that correlation can appear and disappear in nonlinear systems ("mirage correlations") and that shared drivers produce association without interaction (sardine/anchovy/SST example). — [Sugihara et al. 2012 PDF](https://www.science.ntu.edu.tw/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/causalityCCM.pdf)

### Inferences
- Scale of the problem: a typical vessel has ~50 to 300 numeric fields; pairwise scans over lags 0 to N produce thousands to tens of thousands of tests. Without FDR control (Benjamini-Hochberg 1995 [background, unverified]) and autocorrelation correction, a large fraction of "significant" hits will be false, because naive p-values assume independent samples and underway data at 1 Hz to 1 min are heavily autocorrelated.
- Surrogate testing [background, unverified]: phase-randomized (Fourier) or block-bootstrap surrogates preserve each series' autocorrelation spectrum and give honest null distributions for lagged correlation or MI; this is the standard fix for autocorrelation-inflated significance.
- Recommended "autonomous discovery" pipeline (speculative design): (1) restrict to QC-passed data and declared regimes (transit vs station); (2) detrend or partial out lat/lon, solar elevation and ship speed; (3) compute lagged Pearson/Spearman plus a nonlinear measure (MI via KSG estimator or dCor, not MIC); (4) block-bootstrap or phase-surrogate p-values; (5) BH FDR across the full scan; (6) re-test survivors with PCMCI conditioning on the other survivors; (7) check stability across cruises/legs (replication) before promotion.
- Presentation to scientists to avoid fooling them (speculative design): show effect size and lag with uncertainty, the number of tests run, what was conditioned on, a map/time plot of where the relationship holds (to expose single-front artifacts), a "known physics" tag (e.g. ship speed vs intake temperature is known), and a held-out-cruise replication flag. Label outputs "candidate association" not "cause."
- The most useful near-term product is probably anomaly-of-relationship detection: learn stable relationships (e.g. TSG temperature vs hull/intake SST, fluorometer vs PAR quenching) and alert when a known relationship breaks, which points to sensor faults rather than new science.

### Gaps
- Original Reshef et al. 2011 Science paper and Simon and Tibshirani comment were not opened directly; their content is cited only as summarized by Kinney and Atwal.
- No published evaluation of autonomous correlation mining on ship underway data was found.
- Benjamini-Hochberg and surrogate data method papers were not opened this session.

## Q3. Gap filling (linear/spline, Kalman smoothing, DINEOF, MICE, KNN, GP, BRITS, SAITS, CSDI, PyPOTS): benchmarks and pitfalls

### Takeaway
A mature open toolkit (PyPOTS, BSD-3) exposes ~49 imputation algorithms including SAITS, BRITS, CSDI and naive baselines, and TSI-Bench provides a standardized benchmark; but no ocean-ship benchmark exists, and for ship data the safest defaults remain short-gap interpolation and state-space (Kalman) smoothing with explicit flags, with ML imputers or virtual sensors used for longer gaps only into a separate, clearly flagged product.

### Cited Findings
- PyPOTS (BSD-3-Clause) lists 49 imputation-capable entries including SAITS (2023), BRITS, GRU-D, M-RNN (2019 and earlier), CSDI and US-GAN (2021), GP-VAE (2020), TimesNet, ImputeFormer, iTransformer, foundation-model style entries (MOMENT, Time-LLM, GPT4TS), 2025 to 2026 additions (TEFN, TimeMixer++, HELIX, SegRNN), and naive baselines (Lerp, LOCF/NOCB, Mean, Median). Installation via pip, conda-forge, or Docker; models support save/load. Ecosystem: TSDB (datasets), PyGrinder (synthetic missingness), BenchPOTS (benchmarking). Repo shows ~2.1k stars, ~1,883 commits, and reports 1M+ total PyPI downloads. — [PyPOTS GitHub](https://github.com/WenjieDu/PyPOTS)
- TSI-Bench (arXiv 2406.12747) is described as the first comprehensive deep-learning time-series imputation benchmark: 28 algorithms, 8 datasets, 34,804 experiments, multiple missingness rates and patterns, and a method to adapt forecasting models to imputation; code and logs in the AwesomeImputation repo. — [TSI-Bench abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12747); [PyPOTS GitHub](https://github.com/WenjieDu/PyPOTS)
- Runge et al. 2019 warn that missing data may be causally related to the measured process (selection bias). — [Runge et al. 2019, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10105-3)
- Tigramite supports masks/missing values in causal discovery, i.e. imputation is not required for discovery. — [Tigramite GitHub](https://github.com/jakobrunge/tigramite)

### Inferences
- Missingness on ships is often not at random: TSG/fluorometer flow stops in ice, heavy seas, or when the seawater system is secured in port or EEZs; ADCP data degrade with bubbles at high speed; wind sensors are compromised by flow distortion at certain relative directions. This is MNAR, so imputers trained on "normal" conditions will be biased exactly where gaps occur. Benchmarks like TSI-Bench use synthetic masking (MCAR-like patterns), so their rankings may not transfer.
- Long gaps across a moving ship's track are spatial gaps, not just temporal ones: a 6-hour TSG outage at 10 kn is ~110 km of unsampled ocean, where fronts may exist. Temporal imputers (BRITS, SAITS) cannot know about a front; a virtual sensor using satellite SST/salinity or reanalysis plus nav is more physically grounded for long gaps.
- Recommended tiering (speculative design, consistent with common oceanographic QC practice): gaps shorter than the sensor's decorrelation time: linear/spline or Kalman smoother, flagged "interpolated"; medium gaps: multivariate model using co-located sensors (virtual sensor), flagged "modeled" with uncertainty; long gaps: leave missing in the science product, optionally provide external-reference estimate in a separate variable. Never overwrite raw values; carry an IOOS/QARTOD-style or custom flag per value.
- DINEOF [background, unverified] (Beckers and Rixen 2003) is an EOF-based method for gridded satellite fields; useful for filling cloud gaps in satellite reference fields (OSTIA is already gap-free), not directly for 1-D ship time series.
- Compute [background, unverified]: Kalman smoothing, spline, KNN and MICE are CPU-trivial; BRITS/SAITS train in minutes to hours on a GPU or slower on CPU; CSDI (diffusion) is markedly more expensive at inference because it runs many denoising steps. Shipboard servers without GPUs favor classical and small neural models; training can be done ashore.
- PyPOTS fits as an offline/near-real-time library; a trained SAITS model could be loaded in a custom OpenRVDAS transform that consumes a sliding window, but latency and window management must be designed explicitly.

### Gaps
- TSI-Bench's detailed results (which methods win, whether simple baselines are competitive) were not available from the abstract page; the full paper was not opened.
- No benchmark of imputers on research-vessel underway data was found.
- Original BRITS, SAITS, CSDI, DINEOF, MICE papers were not opened in this session.

## Q4. Virtual/soft sensors: industrial practice and ocean examples, physics-informed ML, use as drift references

### Takeaway
Ocean virtual sensors are proven and operational in two places: deterministic derived variables (true wind from relative wind plus nav, salinity from conductivity) and empirical biogeochemical estimators (CANYON-B, ESPER/LIR family) that are already used as reference values for drift and offset correction of BGC-Argo sensors. Data-driven soft sensors for underway ship variables (e.g. predicting TSG temperature from hull sensors, or chlorophyll from multiple optics) are plausible but less documented; their biggest risk is being trained on data from the same drifting sensor.

### Cited Findings
- True wind (deterministic virtual sensor): Smith, Bourassa and Sharp 1999 (JTECH 16, 939 to 952) require six inputs: COG, SOG, heading, wind vane zero reference angle, platform-relative wind direction and speed; plus sensor height above water as metadata. Only 20% of vessels studied reported all six; documented errors include 180 degree convention errors, subtracting rather than adding the course vector, ignoring the zero reference angle, averaging across accelerations producing spikes, flow distortion, and heights referenced to deck. — [COAPS true wind paper](https://coaps.fsu.edu/WOCE/truewind/paper)
- OpenRVDAS implements true wind as a derived-data logger: CachedDataReader -> TrueWindsTransform (a DerivedDataTransform subclass taking relative wind speed/direction, heading, course, speed; conversion factor 0.5144 knots to m/s; `update_on_fields` controls emission) -> CachedDataWriter back to the CachedDataServer. InterpolationTransform supports boxcar_average, nearest, polar_average, and per the docs v2.7.0 adds medians, weighted averages and "window statistics for quality control"; ComputedFieldsTransform handles formula-based values and unit conversions. — [OpenRVDAS derived data docs](https://davidpablocohn.github.io/openrvdas-docs/derived_data/)
- CANYON-B (Bittig et al. 2018, Frontiers in Marine Science): Bayesian neural network trained on GLODAPv2 bottle data; inputs temperature, salinity, oxygen and geolocation (plus pressure/time); outputs AT, CT (DIC), pH, pCO2, nitrate, phosphate, silicate, each with a locally varying uncertainty; validated against independent GO-SHIP bottle and sensor data; comparable to climatologies at large scale and better at 40 to 120 day, 500 to 1,500 km scales; CONTENT combines the four carbonate estimates for internal consistency. — [CANYON-B abstract, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/3fecd4262e04431c98a98b65e604271f)
- ESPER (Carter et al. 2021, Limnology and Oceanography: Methods, doi 10.1002/lom3.10461): estimates phosphate, nitrate, silicate, oxygen, TA, pHT and DIC from up to 16 predictor combinations (salinity and coordinates required); three variants ESPER_NN, ESPER_LIR (locally interpolated regressions), ESPER_Mixed; trained with GLODAPv2.2020 additions; intended for 1980 to about 2030; authors caution against using it to directly quantify surface ocean seasonality. A Python port (PyESPER) exists on PyPI. — [OA-ICC summary of Carter et al. 2021](https://news-oceanacidification-icc.org/2021/10/22/new-and-updated-global-empirical-seawater-property-estimation-routines/); [PyESPER on PyPI (search result)](https://pypi.org/project/PyESPER/)
- Virtual sensor as drift reference, proven case: SOCCOM BGC-Argo QC calibrates oxygen, nitrate and pH sensors against atmospheric reanalysis and empirical algorithms (LIR family), reaching about 3 umol/kg O2, 0.5 umol/kg nitrate and 0.007 pH; the authors present software to automate comparison with reference datasets, used on over 250 floats including all SOCCOM floats, explicitly to reduce subjective visual drift assessment. — [NSF PAR record (Carter et al. 2018 LIR update and SOCCOM QC software abstracts)](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10053799-updated-methods-global-locally-interpolated-estimation-alkalinity-ph-nitrate-lir-global-alkalinity-ph-nitrate-estimates)
- Intake/TSG temperature vs SST: Matthews and Matthews 2013 (Ocean Science 9, 695 to 711) used a 3 m TSG as an analog for engine intake temperature; buckets ran about 0.3 to 0.5 C warmer than 3 m temperature in the central tropical Pacific, with upper-3 m gradients averaging -0.4 +/- 0.2 C and up to ~1 C, day and night; they conclude engine-intake temperatures are "a particularly unreliable measure of actual SST," that engine-room warming explains only hundredths of a degree in their model, and that reliable correction is not possible without intake depth metadata. — [Matthews and Matthews 2013, Ocean Science](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/9/695/2013/os-9-695-2013.pdf)

### Inferences
- Taxonomy for a ship system (speculative design):
  1. Deterministic physics transforms (true wind, practical salinity from conductivity/temperature/pressure via TEOS-10/gsw [background, unverified], derived density, fluorescence-to-chlorophyll with factory/lab calibration, NPQ correction using PAR/solar elevation). Proven, cheap, fit OpenRVDAS ComputedFieldsTransform or custom DerivedDataTransform.
  2. Empirical global estimators (CANYON-B, ESPER, LIR) needing T, S, O2, position; deliver nitrate/TA/DIC/pH estimates with uncertainty. Proven for subsurface; surface-ocean seasonality is an acknowledged weakness (ESPER authors' caution), so use as coarse reference at the surface.
  3. Cruise-local data-driven soft sensors (PLS, GPR, gradient boosting, small NNs) mapping co-located redundant sensors to a target (e.g. TSG temperature from hull-mounted SBE38 plus ship speed plus solar radiation; fluorometer chlorophyll from a second optical sensor plus turbidity/backscatter; ADCP surface current vs nav-derived drift). Plausible; industrial soft-sensor literature uses PLS/GPR/NNs [background, unverified; no review opened this session].
- Drift detection with virtual sensors: the BGC-Argo practice (compare sensor to an independent reference, estimate slowly varying offset/gain, apply adjustment, flag) translates directly to ships. Key rule: the virtual sensor must be independent of the sensor being checked (trained on other cruises, other sensors, or external climatology/reanalysis); otherwise drift is learned into the model and becomes invisible. Residual monitoring (CUSUM or rolling-median of observed minus virtual) on the residual series is a standard approach [background, unverified].
- Physics-informed ML [background, unverified]: constraining models with known relationships (e.g. carbonate system consistency as CONTENT does for CANYON-B; conservation of salt in mixing lines; wind vector algebra) reduces extrapolation errors; CONTENT is a cited example of a consistency layer. Generic PINN approaches for ship sensors are speculative.
- Failure modes: extrapolation into water masses absent from training (coastal, ice-edge, river plumes); front crossings where co-located sensors have different response times or intake depths (Matthews 2013 shows ~0.4 C near-surface gradients, so an intake at a different depth is a different variable, not a drifted one); flow distortion dependent on relative wind direction; sensors sharing the same seawater line sharing the same fault (biofouling, bubbles, warming in the line), which makes them non-independent references.
- Compute: deterministic and LIR-type estimators are trivial; CANYON-B/ESPER_NN inference is lightweight (small networks) and suitable for real-time shipboard use [inference from model architecture as described; inference cost not documented on opened pages].

- Specific CANYON-B or ESPER error statistics were not on the opened pages (abstracts were qualitative).
- No peer-reviewed example found of a cruise-local ML soft sensor used for underway TSG or fluorometer drift detection on a research vessel.

## Q5. External references at sea: reanalysis and satellite matchups (ERA5, OSTIA, SMAP/SMOS, Copernicus Marine) under bandwidth limits

### Takeaway
Reanalysis matchups are a proven reference for ship met sensors: ERA5 has been used to quantify and correct relative-wind-direction-dependent flow distortion biases of -37% to +22% on a research vessel, bringing true wind uncertainty to about +/-5%. Satellite SST/SSS products are natural references for TSG drift but need careful depth/skin and resolution handling; no source opened here quantifies their use on ships.

### Cited Findings
- Landwehr et al. 2020 (AMT 13, 3487 to 3506) used ERA5 10 m neutral winds plus COARE 3.5 drag coefficients and ERA5 heat fluxes to predict wind at anemometer height (~31.5 m) on R/V Akademik Tryoshnikov (Antarctic Circumnavigation Expedition), compared with measured relative wind by relative-wind-direction sector, and derived correction factors. Biases: relative wind speed -37% to +22%, relative direction -17 to +11 degrees; mean true wind speed error +11.5% (5th to 95th percentile -4% to +41%); after correction, ~+/-5% uncertainty limited by ERA5 accuracy. Filters excluded land/ice/coastal data, out-of-range stability, outliers, and ERA5 periods possibly influenced by assimilation of the ship's own observations. Caveat: ERA5 biases can propagate into the correction. — [Landwehr et al. 2020, AMT](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/13/3487/2020/)
- SOCCOM BGC-Argo calibrates oxygen sensors against atmospheric reanalysis as one of its references. — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10053799-updated-methods-global-locally-interpolated-estimation-alkalinity-ph-nitrate-lir-global-alkalinity-ph-nitrate-estimates)
- Matthews and Matthews 2013 show near-surface vertical temperature gradients of several tenths of a degree between bucket and 3 m depth, day and night, in the central tropical Pacific, which bounds how closely any SST product should agree with a ship intake. — [Matthews and Matthews 2013, Ocean Science](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/9/695/2013/os-9-695-2013.pdf)

### Inferences
- Landwehr's important subtlety for autonomous use: the reference may already contain the ship's own data (ERA5 assimilates ship reports), so a reference-vs-ship comparison can be circular. Exclusion or awareness is required.
- Bandwidth-aware design (speculative): pre-cruise, download gridded subsets for the planned track (ERA5 hourly, OSTIA daily L4 SST [background, unverified: ~0.05 degree, gap-free], SMAP/SMOS L3/L4 SSS, Copernicus Marine physics/BGC forecasts) and update over the satellite link with small daily track-window subsets; interpolate to ship position in an OpenRVDAS transform using a local cache (NetCDF/xarray). Only small subsets are needed: a 2 x 2 degree box of daily SST is kilobytes to low megabytes.
- Expected usefulness as drift reference: wind and air pressure (reanalysis) good for detecting step changes and flow distortion; SST (L4 foundation SST) useful for multi-day TSG drift of order 0.1 to 0.3 C, not for small drift because of skin/depth and diurnal warming; satellite SSS useful only at coarse scale (~0.2 to 0.5 psu uncertainty and tens of km resolution [background, unverified]) so detects gross conductivity cell fouling, not fine drift. These magnitudes need verification.

- OSTIA, SMAP/SMOS and Copernicus Marine product specifications (resolution, latency, accuracy) were not opened this session.
- No opened source quantifies TSG vs OSTIA or TSG vs SMAP matchup statistics for research vessels.
- No source on actual bandwidth costs of pulling reanalysis/satellite subsets via ship VSAT/Starlink was found.

## Cross-cutting: OpenRVDAS integration (applies to Q1 to Q5)

### Takeaway
OpenRVDAS already has the needed hook: derived-data loggers (CachedDataReader -> DerivedDataTransform subclass -> CachedDataWriter) run alongside acquisition, so deterministic virtual sensors and lightweight ML soft sensors can be added as transforms that publish new, separately named fields; causal discovery and heavy imputation belong in an offline/batch layer reading logged data.

### Cited Findings
- Derived values are computed by loggers that read from a CachedDataServer, compute, and write back; TrueWindsTransform and InterpolationTransform are concrete DerivedDataTransform subclasses; ComputedFieldsTransform handles formulas; InterpolationTransform v2.7.0 adds window statistics "for quality control." — [OpenRVDAS derived data docs](https://davidpablocohn.github.io/openrvdas-docs/derived_data/)

### Inferences
- Plug-in map (speculative design):
  - Real-time, in OpenRVDAS: deterministic virtual sensors (true wind, salinity, calibrated chlorophyll, NPQ flag); small pretrained soft sensors (PLS/GBM/CANYON-B/ESPER_NN-style) as custom DerivedDataTransform classes emitting `<var>_virtual`, `<var>_virtual_unc` and `<var>_residual`; residual-based drift alarms via InterpolationTransform window statistics or a custom CUSUM transform; external-reference interpolation transform reading a local cache of ERA5/OSTIA subsets.
  - Near-real-time batch (cron or a separate process reading the logger's database/files): gap filling into a separate "filled" product with flags (PyPOTS/Kalman); lagged correlation and FDR scans; Tigramite PCMCI runs per leg with ship-state covariates; results posted to a dashboard as candidate associations.
  - Ashore: model training on multi-cruise archives, CauseMe-style evaluation on synthetic ship-like data, and validation against bottle/CTD samples.
